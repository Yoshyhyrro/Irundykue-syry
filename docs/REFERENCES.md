## Upstream vocabulary

- Coarse map `U` loses information: there are `x != y` with `U x = U y`.
- Second operator `S` recovers what `U` loses.
- Invariance half: `S` is unchanged by the lost "noise". Proved once for
  cokernel-shaped constructions (Lemma 2, and Lemma 2' modulo `d`).
- Completeness half: `S` is unchanged only by the noise. Still proved per
  instance (for example `S-ext-complete`).
- `PathEquivT t` (`AssociatorRelation.agda`) adds to `PathEquiv` the relation
  "arrows of equal type `t` give equivalent one-step paths". For every `t`, a
  monoid-valued assignment respects it exactly when it is constant on the
  fibres of `t` (`Eval.respects⇒ev`, `ev⇒respects`); an assignment that
  separates five pairs cannot respect any `t` with four values
  (`NoGo.no-respect`); what always factors is the word of types along a path
  (`TypeWord.type-word-invariant`). `PathEquiv` alone has no generating
  relation and is propositional equality (`equiv⇒≡`), so well-definedness of a
  functor out of the path category is not a test of anything.
- `CappedResidue.agda` is a complete two-sided instance of the pattern, on the
  natural numbers. The noise is the congruence `~` generated by the single rule
  `x ~ x + M` (for `M <= x`); the coarse map is the residue modulo `M`; the
  second operator is the normal form `cap x = x` below `M` and `x mod M + M`
  from `M` on, that is, the residue together with the bit "is `x >= M`". Proved
  for all of the naturals: `~` is a congruence for `+` and `*`; invariance
  (`cap-inv`: related numbers have equal normal forms) and completeness
  (`cap-complete`: equal normal forms are related) both hold, so `cap` is a
  normal form; the residue loses the size and `cap` recovers it
  (`residue-loses-size`); the fixed points of `cap` are a monoid under
  multiplication (`CapMon`, a target for `AssociatorRelation.Eval`). Not proved:
  that `x -> a^x mod m` factors through `~` for a suitable `M`.
- `L_x` is the integer matrix of left multiplication `y -> x * y` on `CD k`.

## Machine-level notes (checked)

A design note on implementing the boundary relation in machine code was checked
claim by claim with small computations. What holds, and what has to be corrected:

- Holds: Lemma 2 as a dot product. `S^t (x + M v) = S^t x` also survives 64-bit
  wraparound, because a linear identity survives a ring homomorphism. Holds:
  reduction modulo `2^k` is a bit mask. Holds: the index of the product of two
  `CD 4` basis elements is the exclusive or of the indices (all 256 products).
- Correction: one dot product gives one coordinate of the class. A complete
  invariant needs every left-kernel row (the upstream `S-ext` has two). Under
  wraparound the invariance is kept, but different classes can collide.
- Correction: the size bit of `CappedResidue` is not the CPU carry flag. For
  addition it is `b_x OR b_y OR carry`, an accumulated bit; for multiplication it
  is not sticky (`x * 0` clears it); a capped number with modulus `2^w` needs
  `w + 1` bits, so the modulus `2^64` does not fit a 64-bit register.
- Correction: the sign of `e_a * e_b` is not the parity of `popcount(a AND b)`
  (144 of 256 products agree; `e_3 * e_3 = -1` is a counterexample). Use a
  16-by-16 sign table (256 bits) or the recursive definition.
- Correction: in `Cl_N(2)` the commutator of two elements lies only in the `Z`
  lane when the elements have no `Z` component, or when `N = 0` (the Heisenberg
  contraction). For general `N` and general elements the commutator also has `X`
  and `Y` components. The scalar part of the product is `st - g_N(v, w)` with
  `g_N = diag(N, N, N^2)`; whether `CliffordContraction.agda` proves exactly the
  stated formula was not checked.
- Remark: four lanes of 32 bits fill 128 bits; four 64-bit lanes need 256 bits.
  A byte shuffle (`tbl`, `pshufb`) moves 8-bit coefficients; wider coefficients
  need one shuffle per register.
- Design tension: carrying a singularity in a lane or a flag instead of raising an
  exception is a non-trapping alternative to `CO-EXECUTE` with a THROW code. The
  scope of error codes (`docs/EXCEPTIONS.md`) follows the THROW design.
